# Paper experiment programme, 2026-10-18

The repository provides the paper *Generalized Riesz Regression* and its experiments for Econometrica or the Journal of Econometrics. This programme implements `reseach_doc/2026-10-18_experiment_selection/answer.md` in the parent repository, with the author's 2026-10-18 decisions to retain the published RieszNet in E-24, use 20 LaLonde partitions, and include no clipped-alpha arms. Earlier results have been inspected. This is a changed experimental programme, not an untouched original confirmatory design. Existing historical results and the ignored archive are preserved.

## E-25

`23_E25_weak_overlap_moments.ipynb` and `grrexp/e25.py` implement `appdx:att_moment_example`. U is uniform on (0,1), D has success probability 1/(1+U^b0), and Y = D + U + U² + epsilon with independent standard normal epsilon. The true ATT is 1. The fitted odds are exp(a-b log U). All three procedures use the intercept and -log U, with no penalty. UKL and SQ use the identical restrictions a in [-1,1], b in [0.05,0.35] for b0=0.2 and b in [0.4,0.75] for b0=0.6.

The empirical objectives, after dropping parameter-independent terms and common positive constants, are:

- UKL: mean((1-D)r - D log r).
- SQ: mean((1-D)r² - 2Dr).
- Logistic: mean(log(1+exp(t)) - Dt), t = a-b log U.

The data and two folds are shared across procedures. The quadratic outcome OLS is fitted separately in each treatment group on the outer training sample. ATT uses the empirical treated share and the existing estimated-share influence-function correction. Full-sample coefficient estimates supply the primary coefficient quantiles; outer-training coefficient estimates enter ARW.

Reports include the median absolute slope error, central 90% slope-error interval, root-n scaling, the SQ reference normalization, order-statistic Monte Carlo intervals, representer L2 error from one-dimensional integration, conditional bias and RMSE, coverage, conditional interval length, failure frequency, optimizer termination, coefficient boundaries and scaled stationarity residuals. The analytical moment facts are saved without any numerical certificate. At b0=0.6, the SQ stable index is 4/3 and its coefficient-error reference normalization is n^(1/4); at b0=0.2 the reference is root-n.

**implementer's choice for Claude's review:** Logistic likelihood is unrestricted, because the selection restricts the UKL and SQ coefficients specifically. All starts use a=0 and the midpoint of the DGP's permitted b interval; there are no multiple starts. SciPy chooses its default method (L-BFGS-B for the bounded problems, BFGS for logistic likelihood); no iteration limit or tolerance is supplied. `options` remains an explicit optional argument for library users.

**implementer's choice for Claude's review:** The representer error uses the common population treated share and the true distribution of (D,U), to measure the fitted odds rather than sampling error in the share. Its square is the integral of (r_hat-r0)²/(1+r0), divided by pi². An unrestricted logistic estimate outside the L2-integrable range is reported as an infinite L2 error, not converted into a fit failure. Coefficient quantiles condition on returned full-sample coefficients; ARW diagnostics condition on its own returned fits. These two failure mechanisms are recorded separately.

**implementer's choice for Claude's review:** Boundary activity means equality to a supplied coefficient bound, with no invented near-boundary tolerance. Stationarity uses the projected objective gradient divided by max(1, the largest absolute initial gradient). It is descriptive and never rejects a fit. Order-statistic intervals use exact binomial ranks at 95%, allowing unbounded endpoints when the number of replications cannot determine a finite endpoint.

## E-24

The existing DGP and true ATE 1 are unchanged. Six dictionary procedures use the existing raw quadratic dictionary of dimension 56, with treatment interactions. Each outer training sample has three common inner folds. Every GRR ridge is selected from {0.001,0.01,0.1} by the held-out squared Riesz criterion. LogitAIPW selects inverse penalty C from {0.1,1,10} by log loss. The common polynomial outcome ridge selects from {0.001,0.01,0.1} by outcome MSE and is shared across all six dictionary procedures. DML-GBM uses `HistGradientBoostingClassifier` and `HistGradientBoostingRegressor` with defaults and a recorded random state. RieszNet uses the existing `grrexp/riesznet.py`, including its published architecture, multitask objective, targeted regularization and training schedule. It supplies its own jointly learned outcome regression.

Removed: AutoDML-lasso and its import of archived E-15; Nyström features; pilot-based tuning; TMLE estimation; unmatched unregularized EB; the additional zero-penalty and kernel comparison groups; bootstrap comparisons and Holm families. UKL0 with ridge is regularized calibration, not ordinary unregularized EB.

**implementer's choice for Claude's review:** Retain the existing dual offset corresponding to alpha_ref = +2 and -2, penalize every representer coefficient, and leave the two outcome intercepts unpenalized. The raw polynomial coordinates are not standardized. Exact tuning ties choose the larger grid value. Any library failure in a required inner tuning calculation makes that procedure fail; a different grid value is not substituted to conceal a failed calculation. Nonconvergence warnings are failures; other warnings are retained without becoming new acceptance rules. The library messages and evaluated scores are retained for inner and outer fits. Outer evaluation checks only quantities needed by ARW; inner squared Riesz scoring evaluates both treatment values because its criterion requires them.

**implementer's choice for Claude's review:** RieszNet retains its pre-existing sample-SD inference convention (ddof=1), while the other ARW procedures use the existing empirical score variance convention (ddof=0). Its existing training choices remain: 20% training and 80% validation inside each outer training fold; batch size 256; float64; raw covariates; PyTorch initialization; the two published training stages. There is no reduced training path for development runs.

## E-12

The existing DGP and Include and Omit dictionaries are retained, as are the true ATE 1, unpenalized fitting and the alpha_ref offset. Full-sample RW uses the existing `rw_full_inference`, including the fitted-representer correction. The outcome OLS uses the treatment-interaction dictionary of the specified Include or Omit arm. All methods share each generated sample and the five outer folds. LogitAIPW is unpenalized logistic likelihood with the same regressor set and treatment-specific OLS.

Removed: TMLE, evaluation-fold fitting, DML-GBM, BP and BKL arms, clipped-alpha arms, independent EB comparisons, weight-program solves after failures, population influence-function predictions, reference gates, population certificates, balance and weight-ordering tests, and bootstrap families.

**implementer's choice for Claude's review:** Keep descriptive training imbalance, held-out imbalance, ESS and maximum absolute weights alongside inference. Balance tables include the overlap and regressor-set labels. No diagnostic threshold stops fitting or reporting.

## E-19

DGP19Q and its rejection sampler are retained. SQ uses the dictionary (1,d,d³,z1,sin(z2)); outcome OLS uses (1,sin(d),d,d*z1,z1,z2,z2²). Both fits are unpenalized, with the existing derivative functional. The representer can take either sign.

Removed: DGP19L, random features, both oracle regression-perturbation examples, TMLE, population nuisance fitting and certificates, population variance predictions and prediction tests.

**implementer's choice for Claude's review:** The only needed population computation is the target E[cos(D)+0.5 Z1]. It uses 24 Gauss-Legendre nodes in each Z coordinate and 160 nodes in D on [-10,10], normalizing the known conditional density for each Z. The target is about 0.6967143311 and is saved with the run; it is not a manuscript result from a Monte Carlo run. An ordinary unit test compares a finer quadrature, without imposing an experimental acceptance gate.

## E-22

The local LaLonde snapshot and its SHA-256 check are retained. D1 is the existing standardized eight-covariate dictionary; D2 is the existing Dehejia-Wahba dictionary. GRR uses the existing treated intercept and the control dictionary. All six specifications use unpenalized fits and D2 OLS. LogitAIPW uses D1 or D2 for the propensity according to the specification. All specifications share the same predetermined partition at each partition index.

The primary estimate, SE, status and actual cross-fitted-weight diagnostics are reported even when the primary partition fails. The 19 other partitions provide sensitivity quantiles and failure counts. There is no estimate or inferential interval aggregated over successful partitions. The NSW experimental estimate is not used as truth.

Removed: D3 and its tuning, BP, BKL, clipped-alpha arms, TMLE, DML-GBM, independent EB comparison, full-sample weight refits and successful-partition median-aggregated inference.

**implementer's choice for Claude's review:** Partition 0 from the existing fold stream is primary. Partitions 1 through 19 use their own existing fold-stream keys, with no outcome-dependent selection or replacement. These partitions are unstratified, as in the existing design. Diagnostics use precisely the cross-fitted alpha values entering estimation; control SMD uses normalized signed control weights and the existing pooled unweighted SD, ESS uses the signed sum squared over squared weights, and wrong-sign share is reported continuously. The figure distinguishes primary-partition inference from sensitivity estimate quantiles.

## Library computation needed by the retained experiments

**implementer's choice for Claude's review:** Add `evaluate_m_alpha=True` to `grr_functional` and `rw_full_inference`, preserving the previous default behavior. E-12, E-19 and E-22 explicitly set it to False. ARW and full-sample RW need observed representer values and the functional applied to the outcome regression; they do not need the functional applied to the representer. TMLE still always evaluates that functional. This removes an unnecessary counterfactual representer check from the retained procedures without removing any quantity their estimators use. Ordinary tests compare the point estimate against the default calculation, prohibit the unused call, and retain the existing exposure tests for the default behavior.

**implementer's choice for Claude's review:** Record score concentration as the largest squared centered influence-function contribution divided by the sum of squared contributions. The common library records it by estimator, after the ATT estimated-share correction. Calculations are rescaled by their maximum magnitude to avoid overflow; a zero score variance gives an unavailable diagnostic, not an estimator failure. The experiment records also retain weight maxima and ESS. Full vectors are retained for the small LaLonde study; E-12 and E-19 retain the summaries needed by the paper rather than duplicating all weight vectors in every Monte Carlo record. This does not change fitting, estimation or inference.
